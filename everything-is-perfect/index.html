Everything Is Perfect

There's this clip of a doctor who ended up taking care of hospice patients.

Many come in with so many regrets.

So many failures.

And what he said is that by the end, they all seem to realize that their life was perfect.

That it wasn't meant to just be happy.

It was meant to be all experienced.

Life was meant to be lived.

Now I don't know if this was a real clip or ai generated.

I don't think anyone can know anymore.

But I like this idea.

Not of people passing.

But that when they do, right before they do, they view their life as perfection.

"[At that point,] the victory is no longer staying alive, it's integrating everything that's happened."

"And I got to watch, over and over again, that humans in the last few minutes of life, suddenly look back and realize that their life was perfect."

"And they start feeling an ecstatic moment where they realize, oh my God, the whole thing was poetically perfect. All of the heartbreak. All of the estrangement. All of the disappointment. All of the insecurity. I did that for the experience. And I felt everything. I felt everything a human could feel."

"And they suddenly realized it had nothing to do with any of those [traditional indicators of success] metrics, it had to do with the experience of feeling everything a human being is capable of."

I hope you feel everything a human being is capable of.

And I know now, that it was never meant to be easy.

It was meant to be perfect.

To feel everything a human could feel.

I don't wish any of the negatives of life upon you.

I wish only the positive.

But I'm learning, there is not one without the other.

And I've always had this, not fear, but ignorance of passing.

I avoided it like many young(ish) and old(ish) men do.

Have you felt everything a human being is capable of?

What a powerful question.

And remember, no matter what you're going through, when it's time to say good-bye, you will realize that your life was/is/will be perfection.

Just as it should be.

My loved ones should know, I don't need to be near my good-bye time to know, to feel, to believe, that my life is perfection.

I know it now.

Because I have felt what a human should feel.

Maybe not everything.

But it's an interesting lens to view the not so good stuff.

And one I hope you find useful.

As it did me.

My mom used to say "Everything gonna fine." 

In her unique form of English.

She's since added the "be" before fine.

But I like the original phrase best.

My family uses it to this day.

Everything gonna fine.

Amen.
